How money flows (income vs expenses)

Money flow = in vs out

Income: money coming in (job, business, investments)

Expenses: money going out (bills, rent, food, lifestyle)

Budgeting and money control

Budgeting = telling your money where to go before it disappears

Track income → know exactly what comes in

List expenses → fixed (rent) + variable (food, fun)

Pay yourself first → save/invest before spending

Set limits → spend less than you earn

Review weekly → adjust, don’t guess

Saving and emergency funds

Saving = paying your future self

Emergency fund = protection from life’s surprises

Goal: 3–6 months of essential expenses

Keep it liquid → high-yield savings, not investments

Automate savings → remove willpower

Use only for real emergencies (job loss, medical, car)

Debt management (good vs bad debt)

Good debt → moves you forward

Builds income or assets

Low interest, manageable payments

Examples: business loans, real estate, education with ROI

Bad debt → holds you back

High interest

Used for lifestyle or depreciating items

Examples: credit cards, payday loans, impulse car debt

Credit and credit scores
Credit = borrowing trust
Credit score = how risky you look to lenders
Investing basics and compound interest

Investing = putting money to work to grow over time

Assets: stocks, ETFs, real estate, businesses

Risk vs return: higher potential = higher risk

Diversify → don’t bet on one thing

Compound interest = money earning money

You earn returns on your money + past gains

Time matters more than amount

Start early, stay consistent

Retirement and tax-advantaged accounts
Retirement = replacing your income when you stop working
Tax-advantaged = keep more of your money
Taxes and how they impact income

Taxes = the cut the government takes from your earnings

Gross income → what you earn

Net income → what you actually keep

Insurance and risk protection
Insurance = transferring risk you can’t afford
Protects against financial disasters, not small losses

Mindset = how you think about money
Discipline = how you act with money
